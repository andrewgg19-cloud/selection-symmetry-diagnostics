## Overview

This repository contains the focused manuscript and computational materials for the paper **"The Filter Can Also Create Correlations"**.

The central claim is that filtering is not always a neutral observational operation. Under informative selection, filtering can actively induce correlations, deform observable distributions, and generate structural patterns that are not present in the unconstrained natural state.

This work is part of the broader **Induced Dependence Framework**, developed in the companion repository:

[Induced Dependence Framework](https://github.com/andrewgg19-cloud/induced-dependence-framework)

---

## Abstract / Resumen

### English

This work formally demonstrates how the data filtering process induces an emerging correlation, revealing structural invariance across complex systems. Using mathematical homology, seemingly unrelated phenomena are unified: Bell-type filtering environments, satellite-scale quantum experiments such as Micius, model collapse in artificial intelligence, and genetic drift in population biology.

The framework introduces Kullback-Leibler divergence and mutual information as quantitative tools for diagnosing observational deformation generated by rigid selection criteria. The result is a reproducible diagnostic approach for identifying when observed dependence is produced by the filter rather than by the unconstrained natural state.

### Español

Este trabajo demuestra formalmente cómo el proceso de filtrado de datos induce una correlación emergente, evidenciando una invarianza estructural en sistemas complejos. A través de la homología matemática, se unifican fenómenos aparentemente inconexos: entornos tipo Bell, experimentos cuánticos a escala satelital como Micius, colapso de modelos en inteligencia artificial y deriva genética en biología de poblaciones.

El marco introduce la divergencia de Kullback-Leibler y la información mutua como herramientas cuantitativas para diagnosticar la deformación observacional generada por criterios rígidos de selección. El resultado es un enfoque reproducible para identificar cuándo una dependencia observada es producida por el filtro y no por el estado natural no restringido.
